## 1. One-sentence concept

**Food-Fixer is a character-driven, semi-documentary comedy series about an underpaid Night City food-delivery contractor attempting to build a modest life while surviving everyday gig work in Cyberpunk 2077.**

It is *a gaming stream that isn't a gaming stream*: intentionally authored, observational entertainment recorded inside a game, rather than an undifferentiated hours-long gameplay broadcast. The viewer should feel they are following an actual (fictional) person trying to do a very ordinary job in an absurd and dangerous setting.

Naomi brings firsthand experience from real-world food-delivery work. That expertise grounds observations about routing, parking, delays, tips, wages, expenses, performance systems, and the absurdities of gig platforms. Food-Fixer is not supposed to mock people struggling to make ends meet. The comedy comes from situating an unremarkable and recognizable business model in a world that makes its cruelty and indifference spectacularly obvious.

Underlying ambition: the project may become an original creative outlet and eventually a revenue-producing channel, **but no streaming income is assumed or promised**. Protect real paid work, budget, time, and rest while experimenting.

## 2. Creative identity and references

### The intended feel

- **Joov influence:** recognizable challenge premise, severe or absurd rules, genuine gameplay consequences, rising stakes, comedic storytelling, and a reason for a viewer to wonder whether the goal is achievable.
- **Any Austin influence:** curiosity about mundane details of elaborate virtual worlds; serious attention to ordinary infrastructure, everyday life, geography, neighborhoods, economic systems, and environmental storytelling.
- **Naomi's own voice is central.** The goal is inspiration, not imitation. Observational humor, genuine reactions, pauses, tangents, frustration, expertise, and curiosity should remain hers.
- **Approximate performance ratio:** 20% straight-faced, in-universe courier/service-character roleplay; 80% Naomi speaking naturally to the audience. The character framing is useful, not a demanding full-time improv act.
- **Comedy rhythm:** Avoid hyperactive reaction edits, constant screaming, intrusive alerts, fake disasters, and filler clips. Let the city, road trips, quiet moments, and genuine problems breathe.
- **High-effort storytelling; low-budget production.** A laptop-rendered, low-detail Night City is perfectly suitable if the writing, narration, sound design, and edits are thoughtful.

### The central contrast

Night City contains megacorporations, cyberpsychos, advanced cyberware, gang violence, and science-fiction spectacle. Our protagonist has two cheap weapons, a terrible used hatchback, a car payment, and €$20 deliveries. There is almost no need to exaggerate the economics. The *circumstances* provide the absurdity.

**Official corporate attitude:** Food-Fixer regards all disasters as routine operational conditions; its interface is polished, literal-minded, and insultingly indifferent. The corporation must not look deliberately silly. It should appear to belong natively in Cyberpunk's setting.

### Show formats

1. **YouTube: a crafted story per episode.** Typically 12–20 minutes as an initial editing target, with a clear question, beginning, escalation, and ending. Not an unedited Twitch VOD.
2. **Twitch: a live shift.** Initially target about 60–120 minutes only if practical. Viewers influence future orders, locations, and mundane inconveniences. A stream must remain watchable without active chat participation.
3. **Shorts/clips:** individual deliveries naturally produce self-contained stories and highlights, but should not dictate main-series pacing.

## 3. Name, slogan, and company tone

### Selected working identity

**FOOD-FIXER** is the name of the **soulless, consumer-facing food-delivery app and the company operating it**. Night City citizens already understand the word "fixer"; the name is merely a familiar local term repurposed as a dull corporate food-delivery pun. **It is not a mercenary fixer, criminal-gig service, or separate criminal operation.**

Naomi proposed **"We'll get it to you."** as the preferred slogan. Retain that exact simple line. It is vague, familiar, and reassuring until gameplay makes it dubious. Avoid overexplaining the slogan in promotions.

Earlier brainstormed names: "Night City Door Dash" described the concept but directly evoked an existing real delivery brand; "Night City Grub-Get" / "Grub-Get" was explored then superseded by Food-Fixer. **Do not create a second Grub-Get corporation or divide app and personal courier identities.** Food-Fixer is the app; the streamer plays its unfortunate driver. The final channel name/handle is not legally or technically cleared.

### Corporate humor rules

- The app always believes its own copy.
- Deliveries matter more than the driver; inconvenience is always reclassified as a performance metric.
- The system may congratulate V for going into debt or deduct tips for unavoidable delays.
- Driver death, police pursuit, wrecks, freezing, and crashes get polite administrative messages rather than genuine concern.
- The corporate slogan is repeated deadpan over disastrous footage.
- The app acts as a recurring antagonist without requiring an elaborate evil CEO character.
- Keep messages occasional and context-sensitive. Recognition is funnier than an alert every 20 seconds.

Examples and **proposed, unapproved sample copy**:
- "We'll get it to you." displayed above an overturned Galena.
- "NCPD pursuit is not an approved reason for delivery cancellation."
- "Your recent death has negatively impacted your acceptance rate."
- "Your shift has not been terminated."
- "Vehicle destruction is considered normal operational wear."
- "We noticed you have been stationary for 47 seconds."
- "Customer reported missing napkins. Tip removed."
- "Our commitment to affordable technology allows us to pass significant savings directly to our shareholders."
- "Please allow additional time for pedestrians to finish rendering."
- "Our records indicate that you have acquired permanent housing. Congratulations. Your compensation has been adjusted."
- "Failure to deliver does not constitute failure to owe."
- "Thank you for choosing self-employment."

These are jokes and fictional brand text, not statements about real companies.

## 4. Protagonist, game state, and long-range story

### Agreed direction

- **New dedicated save**, not Naomi's normal Cyberpunk character.
- Progress only far enough to make **the whole Night City map accessible**. The proposed handoff is early Act 2 after the mandatory intro/story gating; exact quest-state sequencing should be checked in the actual game.
- Story advancement after that is paused unless specifically useful to the courier series.
- Begin with a genuinely underpowered, cash-strapped V. Frame her as staying on a **friend's couch**; this is a narrative conceit, not a claim that vanilla Cyberpunk mechanically allows all ordinary housing to be disabled. Avoid using any already-owned player apartment as her story home.
- **Initial incident:** Delamain wrecks V's starting Archer Hella during/after the early Act 2 setup. Losing access to that car gives the Food-Fixer orientation a natural plot problem: a prospective driver with no working vehicle.
- **First vehicle:** a cheap, unfortunate **Thorton Galena G240**, funded with a fictional contractor car loan.
- **Long-range ambition:** escape the couch and pay for the shabby **Northside / Watson apartment**, remembered as approximately €$10,000. Verify the displayed price and actual rental conditions in Naomi's installed game before fixing the story ledger.
- The apartment is not a luxurious prize. Its emotional significance is its modesty: a private door, independence, and evidence that V is moving forward.
- Keep the career cumulative. The next shift inherits money, debt, ratings, deliveries, equipment, and consequences from the previous one rather than resetting episodically.

### Potential Season One framing

**Season One: The Probationary Period / From Couch to Keys.**

The season follows a broke employee through hiring, loan-financed transportation, probation, food deliveries, overdue expenses, extremely limited self-defense, and eventual attempts to afford decent housing. The apartment may be a distant series objective, not a forced Season One finale if realistic low wages make the arithmetic impractical.

A future apartment-purchase scene should be permitted to be sincere: V drives there, opens the door, looks around, and the show briefly stops joking. Any corporate punchline can arrive later.

## 5. Food-Fixer employee gameplay handbook

These rules are creative direction; exact exceptions must be tested for playability before publication.

### Core restrictions selected by Naomi

1. **First-person driving only.** All driving/riding in vehicles uses the in-cockpit view, deliberately preserving Cyberpunk's inconvenient visibility, handling, and collisions. No third-person switching to escape difficult parking.
2. **No fast travel on deliveries.** The route and hazards count; you must physically go from pickup to drop-off. Off-shift fast-travel policy remains open.
3. **Very limited cyberware.** Allow mandatory story systems and minimal basic functions such as optics/scanning if essential; prohibit or sharply limit strong combat cyberware, superior mobility, quickhack dominance, and near-invulnerability.
4. **At most two carried/usable weapons.** Early permitted purchases are **white/Tier 1**. A **green/Tier 2** weapon may become eligible only after earned courier progression **and** an authentic purchase with in-game eddies. No looted high-tier bypass.
5. **Don't use the game as an ordinary power-fantasy merc run.** Defensive action is possible; repeatedly farming combat, lucrative gigs, or upgrading into a demigod defeats the character.
6. **No AutoDrive** while performing deliveries, if the game version exposes automated driving.
7. **Prefer no mods that improve the first-person driving camera.** The imperfect driving experience is a deliberate comedic condition.
8. **Keep a separate safety save** before test sessions and major setup steps.

### Proposed specific restrictions still awaiting ratification

- Sandevistan/Berserk, Mantis Blades, projectile launcher, and advanced movement cyberware disallowed.
- Combat quickhacks disallowed or restricted to noncombat utility.
- Healing, armor, and basic implants: to decide after gameplay testing.
- Restrictions on grenades, clothing bonuses, consumables, and cyberware upgrades: undecided.
- Early-game mandatory weapons and story-driven exceptions: record honestly, don't exploit.
- A promotion threshold such as 20 successful deliveries for green weapon eligibility was floated **only as a balancing example**, not fixed policy.
- A vehicle-ownership rule, death/reset rule, accidents, insurance, repairs, and traffic citations need written definitions before they are used for stakes.

**Design rule:** V should be underqualified, not unplayably helpless.

## 6. What constitutes a delivery

Food-Fixer jobs should feel like an app workflow:

1. An order arrives with a Food-Fixer order ID, pickup vendor, delivery location, and optionally customer instructions.
2. Accept order in a custom HUD or simple on-screen ticket.
3. Travel physically to the vendor, purchase relevant food with in-game money where feasible, and stage the pickup.
4. Travel in first person to a plausible drop-off location. Parking, pedestrian access, gang territory, traffic, and glitches are part of the job.
5. Stage the handoff at the door or near an NPC. **Cyberpunk may not support literal place-food-at-door interactions**; be transparent that the food handoff is roleplayed or edited.
6. Record result as DELIVERED, LATE, FAILED, CUSTOMER UNAVAILABLE, DRIVER INCAPACITATED, VEHICLE LOST, etc.
7. Pay V **only when the delivery is successfully completed**, via the agreed real in-game payroll mechanism.
8. Update the shift ledger, rating, delivery counts, relevant gas and loan obligations.

Build a small shortlist of tested, accessible pickup and drop-off locations before a viewer can choose them. Do not let a poll send a driver into a building or location the game physically cannot reach. Viewers may select between options; host can preselect orders when chat is quiet.

**Avoid fake emergent drama:** choose objectives and restrictions, but don't choreograph the accidents. Document actual results.

### Potential episode investigations

- Can a delivery driver obey Night City's laws for five deliveries?
- Which district offers the worst courier route?
- Where the hell are people supposed to park in Kabuki?
- What is Night City's worst delivery neighborhood?
- An entire night shift in the rain.
- The customer who lives on the wrong side of the map.
- Is it even financially possible to survive as a courier?
- Completing a shift with the cheapest possible car and minimal equipment.

The environment itself gets screen time. Observations and exploration can be as compelling as chaos.

## 7. In-game economy and payroll, exact concept vs proposals

### Core decision: REAL GAME MONEY, FICTIONAL COURIER PAY

The gameplay is largely staged/roleplayed, but money paid for completed deliveries should **actually enter V's wallet**. Console commands are permitted **as the scripted Food-Fixer payroll system**, not as cheating money into existence at whim.

**Naomi's latest preferred base rate: €$20 per successful delivery.** Deliberately, realistically insultingly small. A failed delivery pays €$0. Rare €$5–10 tips were proposed, not committed.

Cyberpunk's PC modding framework **Cyber Engine Tweaks (CET)** has a console that can add in-game money. Example command from the design discussion (must be confirmed against installed game/CET version before use):

`Game.AddToInventory("Items.money", 20)`

The fictional wage is generated **at completion** and recorded alongside order ID. Do not grant a paycheck at acceptance or award wages for failed deliveries.

**No CET installed or tested yet.** Command compatibility, negative deductions, game updates, required mod support, and performance on Acer Aspire are not verified. An alternative full pizza-delivery mod was considered, but a much smaller CET-only payroll system is preferred *if feasible*.

### Fuel policy proposal

- Every **five completed deliveries**, V must physically visit an in-world gas station, park, and roleplay a refueling stop.
- Provisional fuel fee: **€$30** per refuel.
- No custom vehicle fuel simulation required in vanilla Cyberpunk. Count/refuel as an agreed show rule.
- Actual fuel counter basis (completed vs attempted orders), exceptions, whether a full tank carries over, and cost need testing.
- The indignity of taking time to refuel for an incredibly small net wage is a feature.
- All deductions must appear consistently in the ledger. Do not quietly invent expenses while claiming the wallet paid them.

### The car loan: Naomi's latest major decision

**Episode One should start with the Delamain/Archer loss and a vehicle loan** instead of requiring V to save the entire Galena price before she can do the job.

Proposed company-sponsored or partner-financed "Contractor Vehicle Assistance Program":

- Principal equals the actual **Galena purchase price shown in the game**, previously tentatively quoted **€$13,000**.
- One-time console loan disbursement for that exact price, with clear on-screen accounting.
- V then purchases the actual car through the game's legitimate car sales system, not a spawned free car.
- **This is a loan, not delivery income.** Record debt immediately. Avoid leftover unaccounted loan money and do not double fund the purchase.
- Provisional **€$25 installment per five completed deliveries**. No interest was agreed. A fixed principal with simple repayment tracking is the first design proposal.
- Loan balance decreases **only by actual principal repaid**. Missing payments become recorded arrears, not imaginary paid installments.
- Payment obligation still arises at a shift's scheduled boundary even if earnings were poor, subject to an agreed due-date rule. Collections/penalties/interest have not been ratified.
- Whether Food-Fixer is the lender or only refers to a sleazy third-party partner is story flavor to decide.
- If later economic balancing changes, disclose the changed rules transparently rather than retroactively altering V's debt.

### Worked example, not a final contract

| Event across five completed deliveries | In-game / bookkeeping amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Five deliveries at €$20 | +€$100 gross |
| One refueling fee (proposed) | -€$30 |
| One car installment (proposed) | -€$25 |
| **Net shift amount** | **€$45** |
| Loan starting balance (illustrative) | €$13,000 |
| Loan balance after one €$25 payment | €$12,975 |

Average take-home after proposed periodic expenses is **€$9 per completed delivery**, before tips, food purchases, mishaps, repairs, and weapon upgrades.

The scale matters: a €$10,000 apartment requires **more than 1,100 deliveries** at that net rate, ignoring all other expenses. €$13,000 repaid at €$25 per five deliveries entails about **2,600 completed deliveries** if there are no extra repayments. This is deliberately oppressive but may be too slow for satisfying on-screen progression. **DO NOT silently inflate earnings, claim it is a quick Season One target, or promise the apartment after a few episodes.** Test runtime and then choose whether to retain long-haul difficulty, allow specific legitimate bonus mechanisms, or alter the show economics prospectively.

### Ledger integrity (important)

- Record **real in-game starting cash** at the dedicated-save starting point.
- Separate initial quest earnings, loan principal, payroll wages, vendor expenses, refuel charges, installments, and other legitimate in-game credits.
- Maintain a simple auditable ledger with order IDs, gross wages, tips, deductions, net, V wallet, loan outstanding, apartment savings.
- Adding money and **removing money** are different technical operations. A command that supports negative amounts was discussed, but **deductions are not verified yet**. Test on a disposable save before production.
- If fuel/loan deductions cannot safely be applied to the game wallet, show them honestly as unpaid liabilities or use a clearly disclosed alternate settlement system. Never suggest expenses reduced V's actual wallet if they did not.
- If deducting shift costs at payout rather than direct withdrawal, never double-deduct. Preserve one internally consistent accounting method.
- Vendors, ammo, and damaged vehicles may have additional actual game costs. Set clear rules for any non-delivery income; story payouts and loot sales should not magically count as Food-Fixer wages.
- V's debt and the **actual available wallet balance** both matter to decisions.

## 8. Pilot: Day 1 / Employee Orientation

**YouTube first, before the initial public Twitch shift.**

Suggested title: **"I Got a Job Delivering Food in Cyberpunk 2077"**  
Episode name: **"Day 1: Employee Orientation"**  
Draft goal: approximately **12–20 minutes of edited video**, flex to whatever the material supports.

### Suggested sequence

1. **Cold open.** A brief real in-game catastrophe or quietly absurd glimpse of the future shift; "We'll get it to you." No need to stage a disaster.
2. **Career pivot.** V introduces herself as a struggling resident trying gig work and staying on a friend's couch. Establish real-world delivery-worker insight without turning the show into full character acting.
3. **Food-Fixer hiring/onboarding.** Present fake corporate training screens; explain dispatch flow, rules, driver status, employment policies, and tiny per-order compensation.
4. **Vehicle disaster.** The original Archer Hella is totaled by Delamain in the game's own storyline; no vehicle means no job.
5. **Financing and car purchase.** Receive one loan for the true purchase price, publicly record a painful new debt, shop for and buy the Galena. Let vehicle selection and financing feel like decisions with consequences.
6. **Test the delivery app.** Show an order coming in, vendor pickup, delivery instructions, disastrous first-person visibility, first drop-off, and scripted payroll *only upon success*.
7. **One or more actual jobs**, depending on available footage. Avoid demanding three if the first alone yields a strong episode.
8. **End-of-shift report.** Count deliveries, first customer experiences, gas status, rating, gross/net, and loan balance. Corporation congratulates employee without recognizing her circumstances.
9. **Cliffhanger into Twitch:** "ORIENTATION COMPLETE. Training compensation: €$0.00. Your first scheduled shift begins soon." This is suggested copy, not a required event.

Pilot's payoff is not necessarily profit. It is a **real career beginning**: by the end, V has a job, a car, and potentially crushing debt.

**Practical note:** The pilot can be filmed locally and edited later. Do not require all Twitch interaction infrastructure for Day 1. Postproduction voiceover can contextualize footage; b-roll of neighborhoods, markets, parking, city streets, vendors, and cockpit driving can make the episode feel authored.

## 9. Live shift format and viewer interaction

### Launch plan

1. **Technical rehearsal** before any public release.
2. **YouTube pilot:** establish app, character, rules, car loan, voice, and branding.
3. **First Twitch live shift:** the next *day* of her Food-Fixer employment; accept real-time audience participation in orders.
4. **Ongoing pipeline:** live shift => review footage => edit a standalone narrative YouTube episode => optionally derive short clips. Edited videos are never merely rebranded VODs.

### Viewer participation to explore

- Select one of a few tested food vendors.
- Select one of several reachable delivery destinations.
- Pick from reasonable or cruel customer instructions.
- Submit reviews and shape driver star rating.
- Vote on questionable detours, special jobs, or route constraints.
- Trigger limited corporate notices or dispatch sounds, **not nonstop spam**.
- Celebrate unusual achievements, including non-successful outcomes.

**Twitch Channel Points were suggested as a mechanism but availability/eligibility have NOT been verified for Naomi's channel.** Always provide fallbacks: native polls if available, ordinary chat votes, or prewritten orders. The show must work with zero viewers on its first broadcast.

### ON SHIFT / OFF SHIFT concept

Keep an on-screen status toggle:
- **ON SHIFT:** cyan/teal courier dispatch ticket, order ID, vendor, destination, status, maybe current rating. This signals *in-character courier/dispatch mode*.
- **OFF SHIFT:** softer magenta/pink variant for open mic/free chat and driver break. It does **not** imply all activities are finished or that the entire broadcast is paused.
- Movement between the two should be fluid; roleplay is about 20%, not forced.

Keep results cumulative: total completed deliveries, failures, rating trends, fuel events, negative earnings, financing, and progression. A recurring **Personnel File** and **Shift Summary** make returning viewers invested.

## 10. Fictional company UI and graphics

Art direction: **immaculate corporate interface over a battered, low-budget, dangerous game world.**

- Cyberpunk-native visual language: dark glass, restrained cyan/teal tech accents, subtle yellow warnings, magenta for OFF SHIFT, thin separators, believable UI typography, slight weathering.
- **Do not** turn the overlay into a neon carnival or deliberately ridiculous parody logo.
- **Do not** dominate gameplay. Compact status and order ticket; small driver cam; occasional notifications.
- Candidate HUD fields: app name, small corporate logo, slogan, order ID, pickup, drop-off, order status, courier rating, shift state, later fuel/car loan status.
- Example callout labels: "CORPORATE ADVISORY", "EMPLOYEE PERFORMANCE INSIGHT", "PAYMENT SETTLED", "DRIVER TERMINATED / SHIFT ACTIVE", "TECHNICAL ADVISORY".
- Proposed webcam treatment: small **DRIVER CAM // LIVE** video-comms frame rather than a conventional giant streamer face box.
- The interface should appear impressively reliable even as Night City's polygons, cars, and people misbehave underneath it.

Brand art and trademark/handle availability remain unverified; avoid using actual corporate marks deceptively. Cyberpunk fan-use rules, copyrighted in-game music/streamer-mode settings, and platform terms should be reviewed before publication.

## 11. Audio identity and Ableton workflow

Naomi has Ableton Live and likes electronic sound design. **Original corporate UI sound effects are a major creative opportunity** and may become one of the most polished elements of Food-Fixer.

Suggested sound kit:
- **App boot / startup:** crisp, optimistic miniature corporate logo theme.
- **New order:** two-note chirp, pleasant and insistent.
- **Order accepted:** reassuring confirmation.
- **Successful delivery:** cheerful four-note chime, hilariously triumphant for mundane work.
- **Tip received:** tiny cash/transaction sound.
- **Tip removed or customer complaint:** disappointed but antiseptic notification.
- **Fuel due / installment due:** subdued warning.
- **NCPD pursuit:** a polite warning tone with disturbing undertones.
- **Driver death/failure:** short declining notes; bureaucratic rather than dramatic.
- **ON/OFF SHIFT toggle:** small distinct pair of cues.
- **Hold music:** clean, irritatingly pleasant corporate loop that can degrade subtly.
- **End-of-shift report:** recognizable recap cue.
- **Slogan sting:** optionally a very sincere voice saying "We'll get it to you."

Make cues in Ableton and **export WAV files**. They can be triggered as simple OBS media sources or inserted in editing. No need to keep Ableton running alongside Cyberpunk. Use original music and controlled audio rather than extensive licensed songs. Sound can develop into a recognizable language: the same happy notification becomes ominous through repetition.

## 12. Running jokes and recurring systems

- **The Algorithm as Antagonist:** Food-Fixer does not employ a visible cartoon villain; its automatic policies are the antagonist.
- **Driver rating:** viewers occasionally file amusingly unfair reviews; five-star rating fluctuates. Build simple, consistent effects if ratings influence jobs.
- **Corporate achievements:** "FIRST DELIVERY", "UNSCHEDULED BREAK", "FINANCIALLY EMPOWERED", "DEDICATED CONTRACTOR", "OUTSTANDING PERFORMANCE" for negative earnings. Achievement messages have **no monetary value**.
- **Financial humiliations:** gas, debt, tiny wages, overdue payments, potential interest or fee jokes only if economics rules are clearly defined.
- **Technical failure canon:** graphical pop-ins and actual glitches may be presented as Food-Fixer technical advisories. Example: "REALITY.EXE HAS STOPPED RESPONDING. Your delivery timer is still running."
- **Vehicle:** the Galena is a character as much as a means of transport: ugly, financed, hard to drive first person, and essential to survival.
- **Customer instructions:** "Please don't knock", "Entrance is around the back", "My dog is friendly"; mundane language becomes ominous in Night City.
- **Unexpected sincerity:** milestone victories, a late-night drive, a successful difficult delivery, progress toward housing. Not every emotional beat gets immediately undercut.

## 13. Recording and performance constraints

### Known

- Hardware: **Acer Aspire laptop**, not a dedicated gaming PC.
- Cyberpunk currently runs at **very low graphical settings**, below 60 fps, but generally feels smooth/playable.
- Naomi owns an **old unused webcam**; make/model and working condition are not yet known.
- OBS, webcam connectivity, encoding settings, and combined game+recording performance remain untested in this project.
- Low-resolution visual quality and occasional natural glitches are acceptable, potentially part of the setting's charm. **Never deliberately degrade graphics or stage crashes.**
- Clear speech and clean audio matter more than texture quality.

### Initial performance strategy

- Test **local recording**, not a public stream.
- First pass: 720p at 30fps as a starting experiment, **hardware encoder only if hardware supports it**, game capture and microphone, no webcam or sophisticated overlays.
- If that succeeds, introduce lightweight static text/images, then short sound cues, then webcam. Test for new stutter at each stage.
- Avoid expensive background-removal processing initially. No green screen is required.
- Prefer simple tools and minimal CPU/GPU burden. Advanced effects, real-time voice processing, and complicated widgets can wait.
- Confirm actual laptop hardware and GPU only when ready to tune settings. Do not infer a specific graphics card or guarantee Twitch streaming performance.

### Very first small test

**Preproduction Test 001: "Does this damn thing work?"**  
A relaxed, approximately 15–20-minute objective, *not* an all-day setup assignment:
1. Locate and connect the webcam.
2. Open Windows Camera; see if the video feed works.
3. Find usable angle and available lighting, with no need to look stream-ready.
4. Record a 20-second test clip and check sound/picture.
5. Stop. No need to install OBS, record Cyberpunk, or publish today.

Next independent technical test: record five minutes of Cyberpunk gameplay through OBS at light settings with microphone. Webcam is optional until the machine passes. **Respect current real-world commitments and sleep/rest; do not turn experimentation into an obligation.** No purchases needed for these tests.

## 14. Editorial standards: how this becomes rewatchable

**Every edited episode must justify its existence independently of the Twitch broadcast.**

- Establish a compelling question or challenge within the opening minute.
- Treat each delivery as a miniature story, but give the episode a larger arc.
- Use cold opens, short narrated explanations, relevant environmental b-roll, purposeful cuts, recurring sound motifs, and an end-of-shift report.
- Let actual emergent failures supply humor. Do not force victory; genuine failures are valid resolutions.
- Edit out empty stretches unless the quietness is itself interesting, observational, or funny.
- Preserve some calm Night City exploration and place-based curiosity.
- Avoid inside jokes inaccessible to someone encountering the series for the first time.
- Don't overuse chat UI, alert floods, forced face-camera reactions, high-volume effects, or reaction-thumbnail tropes.
- A new viewer should understand the company, V's situation, and the episode objective even if they never saw a previous shift.
- A returning viewer should notice accumulated consequences, recurring sounds, and gradual progress.

The show is both **a consistent fictional gig job** and **a creator-led documentary about doing that gig job**, with a modest long-term emotional payoff.
