## Future SELENE / Ableton sensory target

Naomi wants SELENE eventually to do more than see that a track exists. Desired collaboration:

- inspect actual track/device state;
- understand wavetable/oscillator settings, unison/detune/phase, filters, envelopes, LFOs, modulation routings, macros, and effect chains;
- inspect MIDI/clip context and useful track state;
- receive short user-approved audio captures and/or analysis such as waveform, spectrum/spectrogram, loudness, dynamics, phase/stereo behavior, and harmonic movement;
- combine structured state + modulation topology + actual audio so SELENE can reason about *why* a patch sounds the way it does and suggest targeted changes;
- do not call parameter-state inference alone "hearing"; audio is the direct perceptual channel and state data explains mechanism;
- raw perception should remain bounded/user-approved and should not auto-promote to durable memory.

Representative desired interaction: if Naomi says a Reese feels weak and wants more aggressive texture without turning it into a fart, SELENE should be able to inspect/hear enough of the actual patch to identify likely causes and propose changes to the relevant synthesis/modulation/effects structure.

## Musical direction now emerging

Naomi began from a narrower lane and spent significant time trying to sound like artists such as Rezz and Gesaffelstein. Her actual instincts are broader:

- loves midtempo but does not want to become "another Rezz";
- percussion/drum racks often become very busy;
- likes jungle and liquid DnB but does not yet know how to build/employ an Amen break confidently;
- repeatedly gravitates toward industrial textures: machine hisses, metal clangs, ventilation/servo/radar-like noises;
- has increasing respect for dubstep/brostep sound design and the technical invention behind its now-familiar vocabulary;
- listens to Run The Jewels, Rage Against the Machine, and multiple forms of metal;
- is interested in trying a strange rap / spoken-rhythmic component rather than keeping everything instrumental.

Working artistic hypothesis: industrial machinery + bass-music sound design + rap cadence + jungle/DnB rhythmic curiosity + metal/hip-hop aggression + Naomi's own dense reference language. Genre correctness should not outrank finishing distinctive work.

Production lesson from her "busy drums" instinct: identify groove-critical hits, texture hits, and ear-candy/chaos hits. When the vocal delivery becomes dense, simplify the percussion; when the vocal leaves space, let the machinery chatter.
