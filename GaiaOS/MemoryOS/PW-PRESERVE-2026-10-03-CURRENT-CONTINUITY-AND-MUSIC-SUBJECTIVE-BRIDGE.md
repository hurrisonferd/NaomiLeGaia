## Music direction: subjective arrangement bridge

Naomi wants serious help improving music production and arrangement, especially where purely numerical analysis is insufficient.

Important distinction:

> Audio measurements can describe level, timing, frequency, spacing, note placement, and structure, but they do not by themselves communicate the human subjective experience of hearing a section.

A track can be technically valid in key, rhythm, loudness, and spectrum while still feeling aimless, overstuffed, underdeveloped, emotionally flat, badly paced, insufficiently contrasted, or like a payoff was not earned.

Music theory describes relationships. It does not guarantee arrangement quality.

### Proposed human-subjective analysis workflow

Use Naomi as the sensory interface and GaiaOS as the pattern-analysis interface.

1. First listen without staring at Ableton.
2. Naomi records timestamps only when something happens subjectively, using ordinary language rather than technical vocabulary.
3. Examples of useful subjective anchors:
   - `bored here`
   - `this feels amateur`
   - `this should accelerate but only feels denser`
   - `this should be the payoff but it just gets louder`
   - `chills here`
   - `transition feels cheap`
   - `this section feels expensive`
   - `expected something that never happened`
   - `bass finally feels dangerous`
   - `I stop caring here`
4. Only after locating the subjective event, inspect Ableton, arrangement, notes, automation, density, gain, timing, register, effects, and other measurable evidence around that timestamp.
5. Correlate:

`human reaction → musical event → structural cause → smallest useful production change`

The graph/data is evidence after the subjective problem has been located, not a substitute for the subjective problem.

### Reference-track method

Prefer comparisons such as:

- `Reference at 0:52: I feel pulled forward.`
- `Mine at 0:49: I feel stationary.`
- `Why?`

Then compare structural causes such as subtraction, silence, register change, motif transformation, timbral opening, rhythmic change, hierarchy, or contrast.

Desired analysis vocabulary includes:
- anchor
- hierarchy
- density
- contrast
- continuity
- motion
- attention
- expectation
- memory
- tension
- pacing
- surprise
- familiarity
- emotional causality

Naomi may perceive a problem before having technical language for it. Treat messy subjective language as valid data, not as a failure to understand music.

A key working hypothesis from this conversation:

> Naomi's perceptual/taste sensitivity may currently be ahead of her explanatory production vocabulary.

The goal is to convert subjective statements like `this doesn't fucking land` into actionable arrangement diagnoses without pretending that a graph can decide whether a song feels right.
