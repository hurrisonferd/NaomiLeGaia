## Arrangement / composition development

The track developed two primary rhythmic states:

1. **Staggered / hip-hop-weighted groove**
   - feels stalking, heavy, offset, and vocal-friendly;
   - contains Naomi's processed spoken vocals.

2. **Four-on-the-floor groove**
   - began as a solution when the first groove had run about a minute and needed a new direction;
   - turned out to feel strong enough that Naomi considered making it the main or longest section;
   - remains largely instrumental except for the missile narrator explaining that the missile knows where it is.

The important composition lesson discussed:
- two grooves do not automatically equal two songs;
- the problem is two grooves with no causal relationship;
- coherence can come from shared sound vocabulary, foreshadowing, inherited rhythmic fragments, and an A → B → A′ relationship where later sections remember earlier ones.

Naomi extended the middle groove and liked the idea of letting it live longer. The central section's drum complexity rises, then uses duplicated/reversed complexity logic to walk itself back down before risers transition into the original beat again.

The longer alternate arrangement was approximately **3:33** and gave the four-on-the-floor section more room to function as a primary state rather than a brief detour.
